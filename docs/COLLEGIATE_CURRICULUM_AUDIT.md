# Collegiate language-education rubric and curriculum audit

**Audit date:** 2026-09-18  
**Scope:** the implemented eight-lesson LeetGrammar course, with the six planned lessons scored separately as plans, not as delivered instruction.

## Bottom line

LeetGrammar has a **B foundation (82/100)** as a small, teacher-free grammar-and-decoding course. Its sequence, feedback, retrieval, source discipline, and review system are unusually strong for an early prototype.

It is **not yet a collegiate language course (38/100 readiness)**. A college language curriculum must develop more than sentence-form knowledge: interpretive listening and reading, interpersonal communication, presentational speaking and writing, cultural/transcultural inquiry, and performance assessment. The implemented app mainly trains reading-based sentence decoding and short written form retrieval. No planned lesson may raise the implemented score until it exists.

These are deliberately two grades. One answers, “Is the foundation well built?” The other answers, “Does this yet meet a collegiate language program’s breadth and evidence standard?” Combining them would either hide strong instructional engineering or excuse major missing modes.

## Reference frame

The rubric combines:

- **ACTFL World-Readiness Standards:** Communication, Cultures, Connections, Comparisons, Communities, with interpretive, interpersonal, and presentational communication.
- **ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines and Can-Do Statements:** functional performance in reading, listening, speaking, and writing, expressed as observable ability rather than topic coverage.
- **CEFR Companion Volume:** reception, production, interaction, and mediation, including plurilingual/pluricultural competence.
- **MLA’s higher-education report:** translingual/transcultural competence and integration of language, culture, content, and inquiry.
- **Quality Matters Higher Education Rubric:** measurable objectives and alignment among objectives, assessment, materials, activities, technology, accessibility, and learner support.
- **CAST UDL 3.0:** multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression.

This is a demanding synthesis rubric, not an ACTFL or CEFR certification. LeetGrammar has not administered an official proficiency assessment, so this audit assigns no ACTFL or CEFR level.

## Scoring rules

Each dimension is scored from 0 to 4, then multiplied by its weight.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | absent, contradicted, or only aspirational |
| 1 | isolated exposure; no coherent practice-and-assessment chain |
| 2 | developing; repeated but narrow, scaffolded, or inconsistently assessed |
| 3 | solid foundation; aligned practice and assessment, with bounded gaps |
| 4 | collegiate-ready evidence across varied, authentic contexts and increasing independence |

**Hard rules**

1. Planned content earns no implemented points.
2. A grammar explanation is not evidence of communicative performance.
3. Multiple-choice recognition is not equivalent to production.
4. Written sentences do not prove listening or speaking.
5. An internal unit test does not establish an ACTFL/CEFR proficiency level.
6. A score of 0 in interpersonal communication, interpretive listening, culture, or presentational communication blocks a “collegiate-ready” verdict, whatever the total.

### Foundation grade: B (82/100)

This second grade evaluates only the foundation’s stated job: build a correct, coherent, durable decoding-and-form system. It weights sequence, accuracy, practice, feedback/review, assessment plumbing, metacognition, and access, while treating broader college-program strands as required expansion rather than pretending they already exist.

| Foundation criterion | Score |
| --- | ---: |
| Sequence and conceptual coherence | 18/20 |
| Accuracy, sourcing, and QA | 16/20 |
| Practice ladder and cognitive work | 13/15 |
| Feedback, retrieval, and review | 15/15 |
| Assessment architecture | 11/15 |
| Metacognitive teaching | 5/7 |
| Access and product reliability | 4/5 |
| Honest scope/state documentation | 3/3 |
| **Total** | **85/100 (B)** |

The foundation is good enough to build on. It is not broad enough to be marketed as a collegiate course.

## Lesson-by-lesson audit

The grade below measures each implemented lesson as instruction inside the current foundation, not as a stand-alone college course.

| L | Strength | Main weakness | Foundation grade |
| ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 | Clear literacy assumption, authentic sentence target, prediction, gist strategy, retrieval and payoff | One sentence does too much representational work; no fresh transfer text or audio | B+ |
| 2 | Makes an invisible noun property learnable through diagnostic forms and bidirectional pairing | Heavy declarative explanation; vocabulary deck breadth exceeds meaningful contextual use | B |
| 3 | Strong decision procedure and unusually high production share (5/9 exercises) | Large assimilation inventory risks rule-list overload; needs spaced contrast and transfer in connected input | B+ |
| 4 | Meaning-first inclusive/exclusive contrast; links roles, form, and whole sentence | Pronoun inventory is dense; no interpersonal scenario where pronoun choice changes a real exchange | B |

## Planned curriculum audit: Lessons 9–14

Plans can be evaluated for coverage risk but earn **zero implemented credit**.

| Planned lesson | Useful planned contribution | Collegiate gap that remains even if built as described |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9 When It Happens | Adds tense | No narrative/listening task, temporal discourse, or sustained retell |
| 10 Saying “Not” | Adds high-frequency negation | No disagreement, correction, stance, or interpersonal repair |
| 11 Asking Questions | Adds question forms | A question paradigm is not interpersonal inquiry; needs unscripted response and follow-up |
| 12 Describing Words | Adds adjective structures | No descriptive genre, comparison of perspectives, or audience-driven presentation |
| 13 Numbers & Amounts | Adds functional forms | Needs real information-gap tasks, schedules/prices/data, and listening under variation |
| 14 Where & With What | Adds prepositions | Needs navigation/instruction tasks, spatial media, clarification, and transfer |

Even if all six are authored to the current pattern, the course would remain weak in listening, interpersonal communication, oral production, culture, connected discourse, mediation, and authentic performance assessment. More grammar screens will not close those gaps.

## Priority changes

### 1. Make the stated outcome assessable

Replace “understand common spoken Somali and media” as a lone aspiration with a small set of end-of-course can-do outcomes. Do not claim an ACTFL or CEFR level yet. Examples:

- I can identify the topic, sentence signal, and main point in a short familiar spoken exchange.
- I can read a short caption or message and state its gist plus two supported details.
- I can ask and answer a small set of familiar questions, using clarification when I miss something.
- I can produce a short connected message for a named audience, then revise it from feedback.

Every outcome needs a direct assessment in the same mode.

### 2. Add an audio-first evidence path before adding more grammar breadth

The destination is spoken Somali, but the course currently measures text. For every implemented lesson, add sourced or commissioned audio with:

1. first listen for gist,
2. second listen for signal/detail,
3. transcript reveal only after commitment,
4. new-voice or new-sentence transfer,
5. delayed reappearance in homework and unit assessment.

Text-to-speech is not adequate linguistic validation for a tone-sensitive course unless reviewed by qualified speakers.

### 3. Build one interpersonal task type

Start with constrained information gaps rather than a chatbot imitation of conversation. The learner should need to ask, answer, clarify, or repair to complete a goal. Human exchange, instructor review, or carefully bounded peer work may be required for valid evidence. Machine-gradable form entry alone cannot demonstrate interpersonal competence.

### 4. Add culture as inquiry, not decoration

Use short, sourced media or texts created by/for Somali communities. Ask what a product or practice means in context, how speakers position themselves, and what a comparison reveals. Avoid isolated “culture facts.” Record provenance, audience, region, register, and permissions.

### 5. Raise production and transfer quality

Meet the project’s own 60% production target in unit banks, but do not stop at item counts. Add unseen transfer sets, connected messages, and analytic rubrics for comprehensibility, task completion, organization, and form control. Separate learning attempts from summative evidence.

### 6. Extend meta-teaching through Lessons 5–8

The first four lessons now teach a learning move; the later four should teach strategies for:

- listening for the signal before resolving every word,
- unpacking a fused form into signal + person,
- using disagreement between verb ending and signal as a repair cue,
- holding the verb until the end while decoding a long line.

A strategy card must lead into practice on new material, not become another explanation screen.

### 7. Close the quality gaps already named by the repository

- Resolve or remove the seven single-source registry forms and five under-sourced vocabulary entries.
- Add qualified Somali speaker/teacher review, with dialect and register labels.
- Run a formal accessibility audit, including keyboard, screen reader, zoom, motion, and cognitive load.
- Keep implemented/planned status visible in all course documents and metrics.

## Recommended next gate

Do **not** expand to Lessons 9–14 yet. First build a vertical collegiate-quality slice across Lessons 1, 5, and 8:

1. aligned can-do outcome,
2. audio interpretation,
3. reading interpretation,
4. constrained interpersonal or information-gap task,
5. short presentational output,
6. cultural/context source,
7. performance rubric,
8. delayed transfer item.

If that slice works, it becomes the template for the remaining curriculum. If it does not, six more grammar lessons would only scale the wrong shape.
